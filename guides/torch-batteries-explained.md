# Torch batteries explained: AA, CR123, 18650 and built-in USB

The battery decides how bright a torch can be, how long it runs and what it costs you to keep going. Here is what the codes mean.

## AA and AAA

The batteries in your TV remote. You can buy them at any servo or supermarket, which is the whole point. A torch that takes AAs is the one to keep in the glovebox or the blackout kit.

See the [MT20A torch](https://torchoutlet.com.au/products/nitecore-mt20a) (2 x AA), the [LA10 lantern](https://torchoutlet.com.au/products/nitecore-la10) (1 x AA) and the [T5s penlight](https://torchoutlet.com.au/products/nitecore-t5s) (1 x AAA).

## 18650

A rechargeable lithium-ion cell. The name is the size: 18 mm across, 65 mm long. It holds far more energy than an AA, which is why most 1000 lumen torches use one.

Some torches come with the 18650 in the box and a USB port on the torch, so you never need a separate charger. The [MH12](https://torchoutlet.com.au/products/nitecore-mh12) and [MH12GT](https://torchoutlet.com.au/products/nitecore-mh12gt) are sold that way.

## CR123

A 3 volt lithium battery, about half the length of an 18650. It is not rechargeable. It keeps its charge for years on a shelf, so it suits a torch that sits unused until an emergency. Many torches take either one 18650 or two CR123s. See the [P16](https://torchoutlet.com.au/products/nitecore-p16), [P10GT](https://torchoutlet.com.au/products/nitecore-p10gt) and [HC30 headlamp](https://torchoutlet.com.au/products/nitecore-hc30).

## Built-in USB rechargeable

Small lights like the [TIP](https://torchoutlet.com.au/products/nitecore-tip-2017), [TINI](https://torchoutlet.com.au/products/nitecore-tini) and [LR10 lantern](https://torchoutlet.com.au/products/nitecore-lr10) have the battery sealed inside. Plug in a USB lead and that is it. Nothing to buy, nothing to lose.

## Three rules

- **Charge it before first use.** A lithium battery that has sat in a box should get a full charge before you rely on it.
- **Do not mix batteries.** Never run old and new cells, or two different brands, in the same torch.
