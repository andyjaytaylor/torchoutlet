# ATEX on the box does not mean approved on your site

An explosion-proof torch is safety equipment. Buy the wrong one and at best it gets turned away at the gate. Read this before you order.

## What explosion-proof means

In places where flammable gas, vapour or dust can build up, an ordinary torch is an ignition source. Certified lights are designed and tested so they cannot set off the atmosphere around them.

## ATEX and IECEx are not the same thing

- **ATEX** is the European scheme.
- **IECEx** is the international scheme.

Many Australian sites require IECEx or ANZEx certification and will not accept ATEX on its own. Your site's hazardous-area rules decide what is allowed. Ask your safety officer or electrical supervisor before you buy.

## How to read a marking

Markings look like this example: **II 2 G Ex ib IIC T4 Gb**. Each part means something.

| Part | Meaning |
|---|---|
| II | Equipment group. Group II is surface industry. Group I is underground mining. |
| 2 | Category. Category 2 suits Zone 1. Category 1 suits Zone 0. Category 3 suits Zone 2. |
| G | Gas and vapour. D means dust. |
| Ex ib | Protection method. "i" is intrinsic safety. "d" is a flameproof enclosure. |
| IIC | Gas group. IIA covers propane. IIB adds ethylene. IIC adds hydrogen and acetylene. |
| T4 | Maximum surface temperature. T4 is 135 degrees C. T5 is 100. T6 is 85. |
| Gb | Equipment protection level for Zone 1. |

## Zones

- **Zone 0:** explosive atmosphere present continuously or for long periods.
- **Zone 1:** likely during normal operation.
- **Zone 2:** not likely, and only briefly if it happens.

Dust zones follow the same pattern: 20, 21 and 22.
